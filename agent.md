# Website Design and Style Guidelines

## Theme and Aesthetic
The goal of this website is to capture the essence and feeling of the 1830s. The design should evoke an "old-timey", historical, and vintage aesthetic, reminiscent of early 19th-century print, literature, and periodicals.

## Color Palette
- **Primary Theme Color**: Light Green. Use soft, muted shades of light green (e.g., sage, celadon, or pale mint) that reflect nature and historical ink/paper treatments.
- **Secondary Colors**: 
  - Off-white, sepia, or cream for backgrounds (to simulate aged paper).
  - Dark charcoal or faded black for text (to simulate old ink).
  - Subtle earthy tones (browns, golds, or deep reds) for accents.

## Typography
- Use serif fonts that resemble 19th-century letterpress printing (e.g., Playfair Display, Merriweather, or Libre Baskerville).
- Avoid modern sans-serif fonts unless strictly necessary for small UI elements.
- Headings can be ornate or slightly decorative, but keep body text highly legible.

## Layout and Styling
- **Borders and Dividers**: Use classic, simple borders, subtle etched lines, or ornamental flourishes to divide sections.
- **Imagery**: If images are used, they should ideally be styled like woodcut illustrations, steel engravings, or sepia-toned photographs.
- **Texture**: Consider applying a very subtle paper texture to the background to enhance the vintage feel.

## Technical Architecture
**Keep it Simple:**
- Generate only static **HTML pages** and shared **CSS stylesheets**.
- Do not use complex JavaScript frameworks (e.g., React, Vue, Next.js) or unnecessary JavaScript.
- Link the HTML pages together using standard anchor tags (`<a>`).
- Maintain a clean and semantic HTML structure.
